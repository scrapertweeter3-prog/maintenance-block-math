Ten hours a month, whether they happen or not

Shipped software keeps needing attention: dependencies move, an API deprecates, a bug surfaces two quarters after launch. The usual answer from the shop that built the app is a maintenance retainer, typically a ten hour monthly block around $150 an hour, $1,500 a month, $18,000 a year. The block is the product being sold: hours left over at month end expire, and the invoice for a calm month looks exactly like the invoice for a frantic one.

Real demand never fits a flat block. Suppose six months of the year are calm and two of the ten block hours get used; the other eight expire paid, $1,200 of dead time a month, $7,200 across the calm half. The six change months overrun instead: a sixteen hour month bills the block plus six hours at a $180 overage rate, $2,580. The year lands at $24,480, of which $7,200 bought hours that expired untouched, 29.4 percent of the total. Spread over the 108 hours that were actually applied, the true rate is $226.67 an hour, more than double the $105.52 a loaded senior costs on payroll.

The subscription has no block to expire. Poket Dev charges $5,995 a month flat for unlimited requests, one active card at a time, revisions until the card passes, and the work lives in a private GitHub repository the whole way. Maintenance is a few cards a quarter, so the queue holds the trickle for free and the month gets spent when there is a real backlog: subscribe, clear the queue at the 72 hour pace, cancel, keep the code. Nothing is paid upfront to start or to stop. Two burst months a year price the same volume at $11,990 against the retainer's $24,480, and an eleventh change to a card never re-opens a meter.

The card queue and the flat month sit at Poket Dev, an unlimited software development subscription that charges for the month you use and nothing else.
